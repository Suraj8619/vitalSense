# Demo script

Three minutes, no hardware, one laptop. The point of the sequence is not that the waveform
scrolls — it is that the monitor **stops making claims** at the two moments it should, and
you can watch it happen.

Rehearse it once. The two beats that matter are both silences, and they are easy to talk
over.

---

## Before you start

```bash
# terminal 1 — the system
cd server && npm start                    # or with history: see README

# terminal 2 — the patient
cd .. && .venv/bin/python sim/replay.py --record 100 --speed 1
```

Open <http://127.0.0.1:8080>. Leave the central station tab (`/central.html`) open behind
it.

> Replay at **`--speed 1`**. Alarm debounces are wall-clock, so a faster replay compresses
> a ten-second fault below the time the alarm needs and nothing fires — the simulator warns
> you, but the demo is already spoiled by then (D-46).

---

## 0:00 — What you are looking at

> "This is real ECG — record 100 from the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database, thirty minutes of
> a patient annotated beat by beat by cardiologists. It's being streamed at true 360 Hz
> into the same server the ESP32 would talk to, through a model of the analog front end
> and a 12-bit ADC. So the pipeline is seeing ADC counts, not idealised millivolts."

Point at the caret above each QRS. **75 bpm against the record's 75.3 bpm reference.**

---

## 0:30 — The electrode comes off

```bash
# terminal 2 — restart with the fault
.venv/bin/python sim/replay.py --record 100 --speed 1 --scenario leads-off
```

Fifteen seconds in, the electrode detaches for ten seconds.

Watch for, in order:

1. The trace flattens — a detached AD8232 rails, it does not go to zero.
2. **LEADS_OFF**, red, within about 1.5 s.
3. **The numbers disappear.** HR, SpO₂ and temperature all go to `--`.

> "That third thing is the one I care about. The monitor could easily keep showing 75 —
> it has a perfectly good number from a second ago. It withdraws it, because a number on a
> monitor is a claim about *now*."

---

## 1:15 — The electrode goes back on, and nothing happens for three seconds

This is the beat worth waiting through.

> "The electrode is back. The trace is back. And the heart rate is still blank — for about
> three seconds.
>
> That's deliberate, and it's there because of a bug. Reattaching an electrode is a step
> change into the amplifier. It rings through the IIR filters while the detector's
> adaptive thresholds are still tuned to the flat line, and that transient produces a
> burst of detections about a third of a second apart. The monitor displayed **190 bpm,
> tachycardia, signal quality GOOD**, on a patient whose electrodes had just been
> reattached.
>
> Every guard in the pipeline let it through, because every guard was a plausibility
> check — and the artefact was self-consistent. The false beats agreed with each other.
> What was missing wasn't a better threshold, it was context: the system knew the
> electrodes had just been reconnected and wasn't using that fact. Now it throws away the
> filter and threshold state and refuses to report a rate until it has settled."

Then the true rate returns.

---

## 2:00 — The ward view

Switch to `/central.html`. If you have time, run two or three beds:

```bash
for bed in bed-01 bed-02 bed-03; do
  .venv/bin/python sim/replay.py --device-id $bed --record 100 --speed 1 --loop &
done
kill %1   # take one bed offline mid-demo
```

> "Beds sort by worst active alarm, not by bed number — the screen's job is to say who
> needs attention first. And when I kill that device, the bed **stays**, marked offline.
> A tile disappearing looks exactly like a patient being fine."

Acknowledge an alarm. The chip dims and shows a countdown.

> "Acknowledging is a silence, not a dismissal. It expires — sixty seconds for a
> high-priority alarm — and comes back, because the patient hasn't changed, only my
> awareness has."

---

## 2:40 — The evidence

Two files, on screen, briefly:

- **`docs/verification.md`** — generated by `dsp/validate.py`, never hand-written. 22,459
  annotated beats, sensitivity 98.73 %, and the records it does badly on published rather
  than tuned away.
- **`docs/requirements.md`** — generated too. Status is *computed* from evidence: a
  requirement whose evidence hasn't been produced says "not yet measured", and one whose
  evidence says it failed can't be rendered as passing.

> "The last one caught me. SR-07 — 'a signal too poor to trust is flagged, not reported' —
> was marked verified for two phases. All its component tests passed. But the metric only
> existed in Python and the server never called it, so the shipped monitor reported 102
> wrong heart rates on a swamped signal. The fault-injection suite found it in one run."
